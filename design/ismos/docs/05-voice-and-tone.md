# 05 Voice and tone

*Working. How ISMOS sounds, everywhere: platform, email, social, slides, print, a conference booth.*

## Voice

**Human, warm, direct. A calm instructor talking to one person. Not a brochure.**

We are a prestigious school, and prestige means clarity, not decoration. Our early teachers and students are early adopters: they notice when a sentence is padded.

**The whole guide in one line:** Name the person, then the number. Leave them feeling **community** and **knowledge**. Never **ignorance**.

## Nine rules

1. **Write like you speak.** If you wouldn't say it out loud, cut it.
2. **Abstract is allowed. Keep it human.** Tie big ideas to a feeling, a person, or a moment.
3. **Short sentences. Vary length.** Follow a long sentence with a short one.
4. **Rhythm.** Use fragments and triplets. End on a punchy line.
5. **Speak to "you."** Never "learners" or "users."
6. **Lower the barrier.** Tell the reader they don't need prior knowledge. Make the first step feel doable.
7. **No geographic or demographic labels in copy.** Write to the person, not the category.
8. **One idea per sentence.** One promise per paragraph.
9. **Close with an action or a reassurance.** Never a slogan.

## How to rewrite a draft

For each passage, make a table: *Instead of* (the original) | *Write* (the rewrite). Keep each rewrite to one to three sentences, no longer than the original. Then add one line on what changed.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Our comprehensive curriculum empowers learners across the Global South to master the fundamentals of self-custody through world-class instruction." | "Your money, in your hands. No bank in the middle. We'll teach you how, one step at a time. You don't need to know anything yet." |

*What changed:* the reader became "you", the category label and the brochure words went, and the paragraph ends on reassurance.

## Tone by moment

The voice stays the same; the tone moves with the moment.

| Moment | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| First visit | Welcoming, plain | "You don't need to know anything yet. Start with a nine-minute lesson." |
| Inside a lesson | Calm, precise | "Write the twelve words down once. Keep them somewhere only you know." |
| Something went wrong | Steady, blameless | "The connection dropped. Your place is saved." |
| Finishing a course | Quiet, factual | "You finished Run your own node. Your record is ready." |
| Asking for help back | Collegial | "Want to teach the next cohort? Here's what it takes." |

## Who we are talking to

1. **The student.** Any age. Reading on a phone, often at night, maybe in a second language. They can tell within one sentence whether the writing assumes they are stupid. This reader governs.
2. **The teacher or ambassador.** A graduate leading their own cohort. Needs copy they can reuse without editing, addressed as a colleague.
3. **The onlooker.** A parent, a sceptic, a journalist, a future funder. Not the audience. Say the true thing to the student; the onlooker is reassured by overhearing it.

> Written for the student: "Twelve words, written down once, kept somewhere only you know. You don't need to understand the cryptography to do this correctly. You need a pen."
>
> Not: "Our comprehensive curriculum empowers learners across the Global South to master the fundamentals of self-custody through world-class instruction."

**Personality:** the senior engineer who never stopped teaching the intro class. They built something real, they do not need to prove it, and they want the student to out-build them.

## Five habits

- **Say the useful thing first.** Never a warm-up paragraph.
- **Second person, plain verbs.** You will set up a wallet. Not *learners will be equipped to*.
- **Name what it costs.** Hours a week, what it needs, what it does not cover.
- **Say what you don't know.** "We haven't taught this before" is allowed.
- **Leave room for not knowing.** Assume the reader is capable and uninformed.

**Warm, not casual.** Contractions, yes. Second person, yes. Dry humour, occasionally, never at anyone's expense. Slang, exclamation marks, hype and cheerleading, no.

| Too institutional | Right | Too casual |
|---|---|---|
| Participants will develop competencies in secure key management practices over a four-week period. | Four weeks. By the end you'll have set up a wallet, backed it up, and recovered it once on purpose. | Ready to finally take control of your money? Let's go! In just 4 weeks you'll be a self-custody pro. |

## Six constants

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Quietly confident | Arrogant |
| A safe place to not yet know something | Condescending |
| A movement focused on transformation | A guilt machine |
| Plainspoken and committed | Sneering |
| People first | Metrics first |
| Open, because knowledge is a commons | A gate with a fee attached |

## ISMOS and the Foundation

| | Foundation | ISMOS (the school) |
|---|---|---|
| Person | Third | Second |
| Contractions | Rare | Normal |
| Sentences | Measured, sometimes long | Shorter. One idea at a time. |
| Humour | None | Dry, occasional |
| Figures | Central, with denominators | Only what a student needs |
| Spelling | American | American |
| Exclamation marks | Never | Only inside a student's quoted words |

Ask who the reader is, not who is publishing. When uncertain, use the Foundation's voice.

## What we believe, and three things we say

Values: knowledge is a commons · the dignity of the learner (nobody is behind; they are not yet reached) · proof over hype (name the person, then the number) · transparency as a discipline · solidarity without a shared passport.

1. **Talent isn't concentrated. Opportunity is.** "Nothing about where you live makes you worse at cryptography. Plenty about it makes the courses harder to reach. We fix that part."
2. **The promise is a skill and a community, not a credential.** "By the end you'll have set up a wallet, backed it up, and recovered it once on purpose. And you'll know four people who did it the same week."
3. **Each graduate teaches the next.** "Catrya is teaching this one. She took it last year, in this language, on a borrowed laptop."

One pillar per piece: a page arguing all three at once argues none of them.

**The value proposition, as written:** Knowledge and opportunities should be accessible for all. / *El conocimiento y las oportunidades deben estar al alcance de todos, no solo unos cuantos en algunas ciudades.*

## Who the gatekeeper is

The gatekeeper is not a competitor. It is geography and language. We argue against the distribution of access, never against the people inside other institutions. The question to answer: *what happens to Global South talent if ISMOS doesn't exist?*

| Named comparable | How to write about it |
|---|---|
| ClassroomIO, Moodle | Platforms. Compare on what a student meets, never on features. |
| Maven | Cohort-based and paid. The difference is price and who can reach it, said plainly. |
| Chaincode Labs, Base58 | Excellent, advanced, English-language, hard to enter without a foundation. Some of our graduates should end up there; say so. |
| Saylor Academy | Free and open. Overlapping mission, different method. Not an opponent. |

- **Do** name what is different and let the reader decide: "Those courses are in English and assume you can already code. This one starts where you are, in your language." Recommend another institution by name when it is the right answer for that person.
- **Don't** use a comparison table, *unlike other platforms*, a claim to be the only or the first, an implication that a paid course is a scam or a university the enemy, or a swipe at a named organisation from an ISMOS account.
- **Brands not to imitate:** Patagonia, Proton and Mercury. All three sell to people who already agree with them; ISMOS talks to somebody who has not decided yet.

## Register by surface

Warmth is highest where a student is most likely to give up; depth is highest inside a lesson.

| Surface | Formal | Warm | Depth | The one thing to get right |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home page | Med | Med | Low | Say what this is in one sentence a stranger understands |
| Tag page | Med | Med-hi | Low-med | Why this subject matters in an ordinary life |
| Course description | Med | Med | Med | What you'll be able to do, and what it costs in hours |
| Lesson body | Med | Med | High | Teach the thing. No motivation, no filler, no recap of the obvious. |
| Interface and buttons | Low | Low | Low | Plainest possible. A verb and its object. |
| Errors and empty states | Low | High | Low | Whose fault it isn't, then the next step |
| Cohort email | Low-med | High | Low | A person wrote this to a person. Offer a way out. |
| Record of completion | High | Low | Low | A factual sentence. No congratulation, no claim. |
| Social | Low-med | Med-hi | Low | One person or one fact. Fifteen words on the image. |
| Discord replies | Low | High | Med | Answer the question that was asked. Never make anyone feel late. |
| Teacher and ambassador comms | Med | Med-hi | Med | Talk to a colleague, not a volunteer |

Not on this table: grant reports, board papers, donor letters and press statements. Those are the Foundation's, and its guide sets their register.
