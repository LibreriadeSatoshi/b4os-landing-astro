# 05.1 Content

*Working. Words, languages, mechanics and microcopy. The voice is set in 05.*

## Terminology

**Must use:** ISMOS · ISMOS Foundation (in full, first reference, when it is the actor) · ISMOS.XYZ (caps, never with www or https://) · *builders* (mission language) / *students* (operational copy) · *record of completion* / *constancia de finalización* · *freedom tech* lowercase for the field, *Freedom Tech* as a tag · tags in title case, as words, never abbreviated.

**Preferred:** course, not program or module · lesson, not unit · cohort, not class or batch · teacher, not instructor · not yet reached, not behind · finished, not graduated.

**Handle with care:** *Bitcoin* (the root, not the headline) · *Global South* (program documentation only, never student-facing) · *free* (say *free* or *free, always*; never *free to start*, *free trial*, *freemium*) · *AI* (what was made with it, never an adjective for quality).

**Never use:** empower · beneficiaries · journey · robust · transform · world-class · impactful · synergy · unlock · cutting-edge · leverage · best-in-class · the underserved · holistic · at scale · game-changing · thought leadership · passionate · seamless · revolutionise. Never *certificate*, *certificado*, *diploma*, *certified*, *accredited*, *graduate of*.

**And never, in any words:** anything implying the reader was ignorant · sneering at people who haven't adopted anything yet · guilt dressed as encouragement.

**Programs:** Ambassador Program · Teacher Fellowship · B4OS (add what it is, once) · WEACT. The person first, the program second: *Kley is a B4OS grantee*. No program gets its own colour, mark, badge or tagline.

## All languages at parity

We're a global company with the mission to create a space for education in your own language. Every language is an original, never "the translation".

| Language | Code | Script | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | `en` | Latin | Left to right |
| Mandarin Chinese | `zh-Hans` | Simplified Chinese | Left to right |
| Hindi | `hi` | Devanagari | Left to right |
| Spanish | `es` | Latin | Left to right |
| French | `fr` | Latin | Left to right |
| Modern Standard Arabic | `ar` | Arabic | **Right to left** |
| Bengali | `bn` | Bengali | Left to right |
| Russian | `ru` | Cyrillic | Left to right |
| Portuguese | `pt` | Latin | Left to right |
| Japanese | `ja` | Japanese | Left to right |

- Write each version as an original. A good sentence in one language is not always the same sentence in another.
- Address the reader informally where the language allows it (tú, tu, você, ты, तुम); use the respectful neutral form where informal reads as rude (Japanese です/ます).
- Never mix two languages in one sentence, and never leave a button in another language on a page.
- Keep technical terms in the form students meet in the wild (*seed phrase*, *node*, *Lightning*) and define them once in the reader's language.
- Machine translation is a first draft only, labelled BUILT WITH AI until a person who reads the language has checked it. A course in a language nobody on the team reads does not ship.
- When a language is not ready, say so on the surface, in the reader's language: "This lesson is in English for now. The Hindi version is being written."

## Mechanics

Figures for anything countable · numbers and dates formatted for the reader's language (`Intl`; in English and Spanish a thin-space thousands separator, 1 240; Hindi and Bengali group in lakhs, 1,24,000) · always the denominator · never invent, estimate or round up; an unsupplied figure is a visible [placeholder] · dates spelled out in the reader's language (4 March 2026, 4 de marzo de 2026, 2026年3月4日) · 24-hour times with the zone (18:00 UTC−5) · active voice, named actor · serial comma · sentence case · no exclamation marks in interface or course copy · **no em dashes** · **no emoji, anywhere** · report a failure at the same length as a success · first name on second reference · consent before a name, a country or a story is published, every time.

## Interface copy

| Write | Escribir |
|---|---|
| Start the course | Comenzar el curso |
| Next lesson | Siguiente lección |
| Mark as done | Marcar como hecha |
| Download for offline | Descargar sin conexión |
| Save for later | Guardar para después |
| Ask the AI tutor | Preguntar al tutor de IA |
| Get your record | Obtener tu constancia |

Not: *Let's go!* · *Begin your journey* · *Keep the streak alive* · *Claim your certificate* · *Unlock lesson 4* · *Submit / OK* (a verb without its object) · any label longer than four words.

**Empty states:** what is true, then one concrete next step with a number in it. "Nothing started yet. Financial Literacy is where most people begin, and the first lesson is nine minutes."

**When something goes wrong:** what happened, whose fault it is not, what to do next. "The connection dropped, so this lesson stopped loading. Your place is saved. Four lessons are already on this phone if you want to carry on now." No codes in front of a student; a wrong answer gets the reason, never a score.

## Titles

Six words at most, sentence case, no colon. Start with a verb. No level, tier or *101*. No promise about difficulty or speed. *What a wage is worth* · *Check a claim before you repeat it* · *Read a block explorer* · *Run your own node* · *Keep your own keys*. The description: one sentence on what the student will be able to do, then the cost in hours.

## Writing about AI

Say: "This course was built with AI, then read and corrected by Catrya before it was published." · "The tutor can be confidently wrong. Lesson 4 is about checking it." Don't say: *AI-powered learning*, *intelligent tutoring*, *personalised by advanced AI*; never give the tutor a name, personality or pronoun other than *it*. **The sentence to use when asked:** "We use AI to build the curriculum, the resources and the tools, and we teach through those tools. A person reads everything before a student does."

## Worked example

> Kley left Venezuela for Ecuador, then Chile. Four years ago he was a bookkeeper. He took our first Lightning Network course, we took it together, actually, and became a node administrator for a LatAm exchange. Then he started coding. Today he's a full-time B4OS grantee, building the open-source tools that help the next Kley move faster than he did.

At 15 words: *Four years ago Kley was a bookkeeper. Today he builds the tools the next student uses.*
